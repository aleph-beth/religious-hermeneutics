## 1. The kernel read backwards {#s1}

There are texts one reads, and texts one turns over. The story of Babel, in the eleventh chapter of Genesis, belongs to the second kind. Read from left to right, it tells of a city, a tower, a single tongue, and its scattering. Read against the grain — letter by letter, as the rabbinic tradition learned to read — it tells something else: that the tower is a kernel turned inside out.

For the Hebrew name of the tower, **בָּבֶל** (*Bavel*), is the exact consonantal mirror of **לֵבָב** (*levav*), "the heart." The same three letters — *bet*, *bet*, *lamed* — that build the kernel, read backwards, build the tower. *Lev*, *levav*, the Arabic *lubb*: heart, almond, intellect, the innermost and most concentrated thing. Reverse it and you have *Bavel*: the outward, the upward, the self-extending. This single reversal commands everything that follows. It states a thesis in the compass of three letters: **science and technology are a tower of Babel in the most exact sense** — reflection frozen into white modules (the brick, *l-b-n*), cemented by a black matter torn from the ground (the bitumen, *ḥ-m-r*, which is petroleum).

The whole question of this article hangs on a single hinge. Knowledge can remain a **living kernel** — the *lev* that interprets, the well one digs — or it can become a **brick** — a serial module, an inadequate idea, a totalizing heap. The same knowledge, exactly; everything is decided by whether it stays *lev* or turns to brick. From the tower to the well; from accumulation to understanding-by-causes. Let us unwind the thread from the kernel outward, and then turn it back in.

---

## 2. The kernel: *lev / levav / lubb* {#s2}

**לֵב** (*lev*), in its full form **לְבָב** (*levav*, from the root *l-b-b*, contracted to *lev*, *l-b*), is not in Hebrew the organ of feeling. It is the seat of thought — the **intellect**. When Deuteronomy commands "you shall love the Lord your God with all your heart" (*be-khol levavkha*, Dt 6:5), it does not ask for an emotion but for the engagement of the whole understanding. The heart, in the Semitic imagination, thinks. It is the inner chamber where decision, reflection, and counsel are formed.

The same root, in Arabic, slips toward **لُبّ** (*lubb*): the kernel of the fruit, the **almond** — and, by the very same gesture, **intelligence**. The Quran calls the men of true understanding *ulū al-albāb*, "the men of the kernel," those who have a core. Heart, kernel, intellect: a single word for the most interior, the most hidden, the most concentrated thing. The movement it names is **centripetal** — inward, toward the center, toward the germ sealed in its shell.

This is why the kernel has, for its conceptual reverse, the opposition of **husk and almond** — *qishr* and *lubb* in the Sufi lexicon, the shell (*qelippah*) and the spark in the Kabbalistic one. Literalism stops at the husk; the interpreter cracks it open to reach the almond. To read well is to break the shell without crushing the kernel — to find, under the bark of the letter, the living thing it protects. Keep this image in reserve: the entire drama of Babel will turn out to be the drama of a civilization that learned to manufacture husks and forgot the almond.

## 3. The reversal לבב ↔ בבל {#s3}

Turn the consonants of **לבב** around and you obtain **בבל** — *Bavel*, Babel. The kernel read backwards is the tower. Where *lev* moves inward, *Bavel* moves outward and upward: to climb, to spread, to "make ourselves a name" (Gen 11:4). The motion is **centrifugal**, the exact inversion of the kernel's. Babel is a false kernel — a thing built of bricks in the place where the living center should be. It has the form of concentration (a single city, a single tongue, a single project) but the vector of dispersion; it gathers in order to expand, and its gathering carries its scattering within it.

A word of method is owed here, for the reversal is easy to misread.

> **Register: this is *tzeruf*, not etymology.** לבב and בבל **do not derive one from the other.** Historically, *Bavel* is the Akkadian *Bāb-ili*, "the gate of the god," which the Torah deliberately turns into *balal* (בלל, "to confuse, to mingle"). The reversal לבב/בבל is an operation of **letter-permutation** (*tzeruf*) — a canonical midrashic procedure, on the same footing as gematria or *notarikon* — and **not** a genetic kinship. Genesis 11 already performs exactly this kind of gesture on the proper name (בבל → בלל, "Babel" heard as "babble"); this reading merely extends it one notch further. The distinction must be held firmly: **symbolic convergence is not historical descent.** The mirror is meaningful as a figure of thought, not as a claim about the prehistory of the word.

With that guardrail in place, the figure can do its work. The Bible itself authorizes it: the narrator of Genesis 11 is the first to play on the sound of *Bavel*, dissolving the proud "gate of the god" into the stammer of *balal*. To read the tower as the inversion of the heart is to read in the same key — to take the empire's name and turn it inside out until its secret falls out.

## 4. The technician's verse (Genesis 11:3) {#s4}

> "And they said one to another, Come, let us make brick (*levenah*), and burn them thoroughly… And they had **brick for stone** (*levenah* / *even*), and **bitumen for mortar** (*ḥemar* / *ḥomer*)." (Genesis 11:3)

This verse is the most technical in the primeval history, and it is built on a precise device. To every material **given by nature** the text opposes its **manufactured double**, which sounds almost the same: *levenah* against *even* (brick against stone), *ḥemar* against *ḥomer* (bitumen against mortar). The language of Babel is the production of the **artifact that imitates the thing** — the made object that takes the place of the given one and answers to its name by a single shifted vowel. Stone becomes brick; mortar becomes bitumen; nature is doubled by industry, and the double is what the builders actually handle.

The verse is saturated, too, with a single phonetic cell: the sequence **l-b**. *Nilbenah levenim* (*l-b-n*, "let us make bricks"), *Bavel* (*b-b-l*), *balal* (*b-l-l*) — an alliterative storm circling a center that is never named: *lev* (*l-b-b*), the absent heart. The whole passage murmurs the consonants of the kernel while building the tower; the heart is present everywhere as sound and absent everywhere as meaning. And fire is named — "let us burn them thoroughly" (*nisrefah lisrefah*) — for cooking is the first technology, the heat that turns soft clay into a fixed, reproducible unit. Before the tower rises, the kiln has already done its quiet, decisive work.

## 5. The white brick: frozen reflection {#s5}

**לְבֵנָה** (*levenah*), "brick," comes from the root **l-b-n**, which is also the root of **לָבָן** (*lavan*), "white." The brick is, etymologically, the **white** thing — and this whiteness is not innocent. The French and English word *reflection* gathers what Hebrew keeps apart: a white surface *reflects* light, and the *lev* *reflects* in the sense of thought. The brick is therefore, quite literally, **reflection** — but reflection **frozen**: poured into the mold, fired in the kiln, reproduced identically a thousand times over. A thought that has become an interchangeable module. The mind's act of mirroring the world is cast in clay and set out in courses.

Here a midrash sharpens the point to its cruelest edge.

> **Midrash (Tanḥuma, on the builders of Babel).** "If a brick fell, they wept; if a man fell, they did nothing." The reflection-brick has replaced the man-heart. The unit of construction has become more precious than the living center it was meant to serve. And note where the brick belongs in Babylonian civilization: the clay tablet is the support of **writing**. The brick is the cousin of the cuneiform tablet — standardized, fired, inscribed, and multiplied. Against it the tradition sets the **Tables of stone of Sinai** (*luḥot even*), **given and not manufactured**, of which the Zohar says that the tower is the parody. On one side, the module that men bake and stack to make their own name; on the other, the stone that is received, carrying a name not their own.

The opposition is exact and total. The brick is fabricated, uniform, horizontal, anonymous, multipliable; the tablet of the Law is given, singular, vertical, signed, unique. Babel writes on what it makes; Sinai receives what is written. The white brick is reflection that has stopped reflecting — light bounced off a dead surface, thought that no longer thinks because it has been cooked into a thing.

## 6. The genealogical stone: *even* = *av* + *ben* {#s6}

To the brick the verse opposes **stone**, *even* — and the stone hides a classical, received *tzeruf*. **אֶבֶן** (*even*) reads as **אָב** (*av*, "father") plus **בֵּן** (*ben*, "son"), telescoped onto the *bet* they share. The stone **carries the lineage**: in its very letters it holds father and son, relation, transmission, generation. It is **vertical in time** — it binds what came before to what comes after. To build on stone is to build on what is handed down.

The brick (*levenah*) is the exact opposite: a serial clone with neither father nor son, **horizontal and flat**. One brick is interchangeable with the next; none transmits anything but its own copy. Babel replaces the stone-that-carries-the-generations with the brick-that-carries-only-its-replica. This is why the Law is engraved on *even* — received, transmissible, bearing the lineage of those who pass it on — while Babel heaps up only anonymous *levenim*, modules without ancestry or posterity. The choice of building material is a choice between two relations to time: the genealogical and the serial, the inherited and the manufactured.

*(A note on color: the stone is not black but **grey** — the non-color of the raw given, of what is found rather than made. The only black in the whole scene is the bitumen. Hold that distinction; it will matter when we come to the black matter that binds the white bricks.)*

## 7. The bitumen: *ḥ-m-r*, matter without form {#s7}

If the brick is a meditation on the module, the bitumen is a meditation on **matter itself** — and the root **ḥ-m-r** is, on its own, a small treatise. Follow its branches:

- **ḥomer** — mortar, the binding paste; **but also** "matter" in the technical, philosophical sense: the *hylē* that the medieval translators (Ibn Tibbon rendering Maimonides) opposed to **form** (*tzurah*). *Ḥomer* is prime matter, the formless stuff awaiting shape.
- **ḥamor** — the donkey, the beast of burden, the most material of animals, all body and labor.
- **ḥamar** — to ferment, to seethe; whence **ḥemer**, wine (Aramaic *ḥamra*, Arabic *khamr*).

Read the chain as a single sentence and it tells the whole story: man masters matter (*ḥomer*), he binds it (*ḥemar*), it ferments into intoxication (*ḥemer*), and he who took himself for the master ends as the beast (*ḥamor*). The would-be potter is undone by his own clay. For this is the precise theological formula of Babel: **Babel is *ḥomer* without *tzurah*** — the mastery of matter without form, the handling of stuff that has refused to receive shape from above. It is the inversion of the prophetic image of Isaiah and Jeremiah: "we are the clay, and You are the potter" (Is 64:7; Jr 18:6). At Babel the clay declares itself the potter. The matter that should have been informed by a higher form sets out to give itself its own — and gives itself, in the end, only fermentation and the donkey.

---

## 8. The literal irony: bitumen is petroleum {#s8}

Here the figure stops being a figure and becomes a fact. The black matter that the Mesopotamians scraped from the natural asphalt seeps to bind their ziggurats — the **Etemenanki**, the great tower of Babylon that tradition remembers as Babel — is, once refined, the **hydrocarbon** that runs industrial civilization. The bitumen of Genesis 11:3 and the oil of the twentieth century are the same substance, the same black ooze drawn from the same ground. **Babel was already a civilization of petroleum.**

Read the verse once more with this in view, and it resolves into something startling: word for word, Genesis 11:3 is a **list of the materials of an industrial revolution**. The **fire** (the kiln, the furnace); the **manufactured module** (the brick, the standardized component); the **chemical binder extracted from the soil** (the bitumen, the hydrocarbon). Heat, the mass-produced unit, and the fossil cement that holds it together — the three pillars of any industrial order, set down in a single biblical verse and placed by the tradition under the reign of **Nimrod**, the first king, power become pure technique. The tower is not a fairy tale about hubris reaching for the sky. It is a remarkably exact inventory of what it takes to build at scale: standardize the unit, fire it, and glue it with oil.

## 9. Science as a tower of Babel {#s9}

We can now state the thesis without metaphor, because the metaphor has collapsed into description. Science and technology are a tower of Babel **in the exact sense**: reflection frozen into white modules, cemented by a black matter torn from the earth. A standardized cognition that runs on hydrocarbon.

Consider how literally each term applies. The **white brick** is the unit of knowledge made interchangeable: the reproducible result, the standardized procedure, the module that any laboratory can cast from the same mold and stack onto the courses already laid. Each brick is, by its very etymology, *reflection* — genuine thought, really mirrored from the world — and yet *frozen*: cooked into a citable, transferable, identical block. The *lev* is therefore **everywhere present as a trace** — every white brick is reflected thought — and **everywhere absent as a living heart**, because it has been molded, fired, and multiplied past the point where it still thinks. And the whole edifice is bound by literal hydrocarbon: the energy that powers the machines, the laboratories, the servers, the global coordination that lets the tower keep rising. The science-Babel is reflection cemented by oil, exactly as Genesis describes.

This is not a condemnation of knowledge. It is a diagnosis of a **state** that knowledge can fall into — the state in which it ceases to be a kernel and becomes a heap. Whether that fall is fatal is the question of the conclusion.

## 10. The two stories and the third way {#s10}

The equivocation of Babel has two stories — and they are its husk and its almond.

**The spatial story (literalist).** God is "on high"; the tower seeks to reach Him by altitude. This is, first of all, a **hermeneutic** error: taking ontological height for a geographical coordinate, mistaking the "above" of transcendence for a place one could climb to. It is precisely the anthropomorphism of *place* that Maimonides dismantles throughout the *Guide of the Perplexed*: God is not "in" a location, and the elevation Scripture ascribes to Him is one of rank and being, not of metres. On this reading the tower is the **monument of literalism** — the architecture of a misread metaphor. One does not reach by a ladder what is not in any place. Babel fails because it took a figure of speech for a survey map.

**The almond story (*scientia Dei*).** But there is a second reading, and it does not stop at the husk. To reach God is not to climb but to **know as God knows**. Maimonides: God knows things by knowing Himself as their cause — divine knowledge is not a gaze cast upon objects but the self-knowledge of the source from which they flow. Spinoza pushes the thought to its limit: the *scientia intuitiva*, the third kind of knowledge, grasps things *sub specie aeternitatis* and so participates in the knowledge God has of Himself; the *amor Dei intellectualis* by which we understand Him **is** the very love with which God loves Himself (*Ethics* V). *Deus sive Natura*: to do physics is to read the attributes — thought and extension. On this reading, "to arrive at science as God understands it" is not blasphemy but the **definition of the highest good**. The true summit is not above the builders' heads. It is inside.

**The vertigo.** And here the modern world does something the Babylonians could only mime: technology accomplishes *both readings at once, literally.* The rocket has physically reached the sky — the spatial tower is built, and it works. And physics has conquered the total overview, the gaze *sub specie aeternitatis*, the view of the whole from outside that the ziggurat-builders only gestured at. **Babel, at last, succeeds.** But does it reach the living God, or build His perfect counterfeit? Everything now depends on distinguishing two ways of "knowing as God knows":

- **Understanding by causes** — the adequate idea, the well one digs — which is a *real participation* in divine knowing. To grasp why a thing must be as it is, from its cause, is to share, in our small measure, the knowledge the cause has of itself.
- **Totalizing accumulation** — knowledge-as-power, a de-facto omniscience heaped up in modules — which is an **inadequate idea** of divine science: the sign of omniscience without its life, the overview frozen. It has the extension of God's knowledge and none of its interiority. It mistakes **height for depth.**

**Water decides.** One does not reach the *scientia Dei* by climbing (accumulation) but by descending (understanding) — **not the tower, but the well** (*be'er*, "to dig"). The image is precise: the tower adds course upon course outward and upward, always toward the surface; the well removes earth, going down toward the source, toward the water that was there before any building. The *amor Dei* is a well, not a tower. To understand is to dig until you reach the spring; to accumulate is to stack until you block out the sky.

**The contemporary *balal*.** And the old curse has not lifted; it has only changed scale. The tower of knowledge bursts today into a thousand specialized tongues, mutually unintelligible — each discipline a dialect no neighboring discipline can read. The dream of a final single idiom, the "theory of everything," the capstone brick that would crown the whole, carries its own dispersion within it: the higher the tower climbs, the fewer can read it entire, until no one holds the whole and the *balal* is complete. The confusion of tongues is not behind us in myth; it is the daily condition of a science too tall for any single mind.

> **The two deaths of the letter.** The **brick** is the letter **frozen**; *balal* is the letter **shattered**. Between the two remains the one thing that is neither baked nor broken: the **lev that interprets** — the kernel that is not a module. Midrash is the living **third way**, the refusal of both deaths: against the frozen letter of the literalist and the shattered letter of the babble, the heart that cracks the husk and keeps the almond alive.

**Synthesis.** The science-brick — reflection frozen, cemented with bitumen, raised to "make a name" — is Babel consummated: the inadequate idea of divine knowledge, doomed to *balal*. The science-*lev* — the living understanding of causes, *sub specie aeternitatis* — is Babel redeemed: the well and not the tower, the *amor Dei intellectualis*. It is the **same knowledge, exactly**; everything is decided by this alone — that it remain *lev* or become brick. Yes, one can arrive at science as God understands it — on the one condition that one **digs the well, and does not build the tower.**

## 11. Bibliographical references {#s11}

- **Maimonides**, *The Guide of the Perplexed*, esp. Part I (on divine names, place, and anthropomorphism) and Part III (on providence and divine knowledge) — the critique of the anthropomorphism of place and the doctrine that God knows things by knowing Himself as their cause.
- **Spinoza**, *Ethics*, Part V — the *scientia intuitiva* (third kind of knowledge), the *amor Dei intellectualis*, and the identity of our love of God with God's love of Himself; *Deus sive Natura*.
- **The Zohar** and the Tanḥuma midrash on Genesis 11 — the tower as parody of Sinai; "if a brick fell they wept, if a man fell they did nothing."
- **Genesis 11:1-9** — the primary text (the bricks, the bitumen, *Bavel* / *balal*); **Isaiah 64:7** and **Jeremiah 18:6** — "we are the clay, You are the potter."
- **A. Parrot**, *Ziggurats et tour de Babel*, Paris, 1949, and the archaeology of the **Etemenanki** — bitumen as binder in Mesopotamian construction.
- **G. Steiner**, *After Babel: Aspects of Language and Translation*, Oxford University Press, 1975 — the confusion of tongues as the permanent condition of language and translation.
- On the philosophical vocabulary of *ḥomer* / *tzurah* (matter / form): **Ibn Tibbon**'s Hebrew renderings of the Aristotelian *hylē* and *morphē* in the translation of the *Guide*.

*Note: simplified transcriptions of Hebrew, Aramaic, Arabic, and Akkadian. The reversal לבב / בבל is a midrashic figure (*tzeruf*), not a historical etymology.*
