**Type**: Structural analysis
**Thesis**: The Europe-Islam relationship between the 12th and 20th centuries follows an invariant three-phase pattern: reception of knowledge -> expulsion of transmitters -> conquest with the tools received.

---

## 1. Phase 1: Reception (12th-15th centuries) {#s1}

**Mechanism**: Europe translates, copies, and assimilates Arabo-Andalusian knowledge. The entry points are Toledo (reconquered in 1085), Palermo (Norman Sicily), Montpellier, and Salerno. The translators are predominantly Jewish — trilingual intermediaries (Arabic-Hebrew-Latin) between two worlds at war.

**Transmitted knowledge**:

| Field | Knowledge | Source |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Philosophy | Aristotle + commentaries by Averroes | Cordoba -> Toledo -> Paris |
| Astronomy | Astrolabe, astronomical tables | Al-Zarqali (Cordoba) -> Zacuto |
| Mathematics | Algebra, algorithm, zero, base 10 | Al-Khwarizmi (Baghdad) -> Al-Andalus |
| Navigation | Lateen sail, portolan cartography | Arab dhows -> caravel |
| Military | Gunpowder, artillery | China -> Hasan al-Rammah -> Al-Andalus |
| Medicine | Avicenna's Canon, surgery of Abulcasis | Translated in Toledo and Salerno |
| Optics | Treatise by Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen) | Translated in the 13th century |
| Chemistry | Distillation, pharmacology | *Al-kimiya'* -> alchemy -> chemistry |

**Duration**: ~300 years of systematic transfer.

## 2. Phase 2: Expulsion (1492-1609) {#s2}

**Mechanism**: Once the knowledge is assimilated, Europe expels the communities that transmitted it.

| Date | Event | Consequence |
|------|-------|-------------|
| 1492 | Expulsion of the Jews from Spain (Alhambra Decree) | 200,000 people, end of 1,000 years of presence |
| 1492 | Fall of Granada | End of the last Muslim state in Iberia |
| 1497 | Expulsion of the Jews from Portugal | Zacuto himself goes into exile |
| 1502 | Forced conversion of the Muslims of Castile | End of legal Islamic practice |
| 1609 | Expulsion of the Moriscos | ~300,000 people toward the Maghreb |

**Key point**: The expulsion comes *after* the assimilation. Europe closes the door after emptying the house.

## 3. Phase 3: Conquest (16th-20th centuries) {#s3}

**Mechanism**: Europe turns the tools it received against those who forged them.

| Conquest | Tools of Islamic origin |
|----------|------------------------|
| Americas (1492-1550) | Navigation (astrolabe), gunpowder, cartography |
| Portuguese India (1510) | Zacuto's tables, naval artillery |
| Ottoman Constantinople resists then weakens | Against a Europe strengthened by its own tools |
| Egypt (1798) | Artillery (Arabo-Chinese gunpowder), science |
| Algeria (1830) | Artillery, ships |
| Tunisia (1881), Morocco (1912) | Overall technological superiority |
| Middle East (mandates, 1920) | The full arsenal |

## 4. The structural paradox {#s4}

The reception-expulsion-conquest pattern is not unique to the Europe-Islam relationship — it is a law of knowledge transmission:

- The **Greeks** transmit their philosophy to the **Arabs** -> the Arabs build a rival civilization
- The **Arabs** transmit that philosophy to the **Europeans** -> Europe colonizes the Arab world
- **Europe** transmits its technology to the **colonies** -> the colonies liberate themselves (20th century)

Knowledge is a weapon that never remains in the hands of its creator.
