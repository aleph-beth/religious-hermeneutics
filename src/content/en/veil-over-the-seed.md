The Arabic word *kāfir*, traditionally translated as "infidel," originally means the sower who covers the seed with earth: to cover, to veil, to conceal. This etymology imposes a question on any analysis of the contemporary Arab world: what, today, is being covered? The documented answer of this article is difference itself — religious difference, ethnic difference, difference of color, difference of history. From blasphemy laws to the Yazidi genocide, from the anti-African xenophobia of the Maghreb to the measured spread of antisemitism in Arabic, a configuration repeats with sufficient regularity to be called a system.

Yet this configuration is by no means necessary. The Islam of the origins is an ethic of justice founded on individual choice: no coercion in religion, responsibility of the person, primacy of judgment. It is a certain number of currents and practices — not the faith itself — that metamorphosed this ethic into an ideology of hatred and communal closure.

The plan is as follows. The first part establishes what the Islam of the origins was: a religion of choice and justice. The second part isolates the mechanisms of the metamorphosis: how, in certain currents, faith became ideology. The third part documents four constellations of the hatred of difference, with their figures and their sources. The fourth part sets out the methodological fork that alone makes it possible to conclude without essentializing. The conclusion opens onto the question of deradicalization.

---

## 1. The Islam of the Origins: An Ethic of Justice and Choice {#s1}

Three features define early Islam as far as the sources allow us to establish it.

**First feature: freedom of religious choice is a founding principle, not a late concession.** The verse "No coercion in religion" (Qur'an 2:256) states a structural rule: faith has value only if chosen; an imposed faith is not faith but submission, which the text precisely calls hypocrisy. Direct corollary: responsibility is individual. The Qur'an repeatedly rejects collective responsibility (each soul is answerable only for its own acts), which ruins in advance any theology of the scapegoat.

**Second feature: justice is the purpose of the law, not its pretext.** The mission of the prophets is explicitly defined as the establishment of justice (Qur'an 57:25); justice toward the stranger, the widow, and the orphan is the recurrent criterion of authentic piety. A law that persecutes difference contradicts its own declared purpose.

**Third feature: the Islam of the first centuries produced a tradition of rational debate.** The Mu'tazilite current defended free will and the rational knowability of truth; the *kalām*, philosophy (*falāsifa*), and the very existence of multiple legal schools (four Sunni, plus the Shiite and Ibadi currents) attest that orthodoxy was never single enough to suppress divergence. Disagreement was constitutive, not pathological.

These three features form a coherent system: a chosen faith, judged by justice, living within recognized disagreement. The persecution of difference contradicts each of them.

## 2. The Metamorphosis: From Faith to Ideology {#s2}

Preliminary definition: a faith is a relation between a person and a truth; an ideology is a program of power that mobilizes the sacred for other ends. The metamorphosis documented in certain contemporary currents of thought operates through three mechanisms.

**First mechanism: the transfer of responsibility from the subject to the community.** The Qur'an founds individual responsibility; communal ideologies replace it with a responsibility of belonging — one is guilty of being Jewish, Christian, Black, Shiite, Yazidi, women included. This reversal is exactly the negation of the original principle, and it is verifiable in the laws: article 98(f) of the Egyptian Penal Code (amended by law 147/2006) criminalizes blasphemy and is in fact used to prosecute converts; Saudi jurisprudence, in the absence of a codified penal code, maintains the death penalty for apostasy (the Hamza Kashgari case, 2012). Religious choice, a founding freedom, becomes a crime of the state.

**Second mechanism: the fusion of the religious and the political.** Contemporary ideologies — state Wahhabism, the Islamism of the Muslim Brotherhood, Khomeinism — restructure faith as a program for taking power. The congress of justice gives way to the fight against the internal enemy: the minoritarian, the different, the heretic. *Takfir* — declaring another person an unbeliever, hence outside the community and the protection of the law — becomes the central instrument. The nuance is decisive: *takfir* is not an excess of religion, it is a political weapon in religious coating, which turns the vocabulary of faith against its own ethic.

**Third mechanism: de-historicization.** Communal ideologies present their practices of persecution as eternal Islam, erasing the diversity of the schools, the relative tolerance of certain periods, and the regional traditions of coexistence. Whoever knows the history knows that sharia was first a discussion among jurists, not a single repressive code; whoever does not know it believes that hatred was always there.

These three mechanisms — transfer of responsibility, politico-religious fusion, de-historicization — produce the same result: an ideology that calls itself religion and functions as a machine for producing the outside.

## 3. Documented Findings: Four Constellations of the Hatred of Difference {#s3}

The data that follow are presented with their level of proof, in accordance with the method: each figure is worth what its production protocol is worth.

### States and their laws

The 2026 annual report of the USCIRF (covering the year 2025) recommends eighteen countries as "countries of particular concern" — including Saudi Arabia, Iran, Libya, and Syria — and eleven more on the watch list, including Algeria, Egypt, Iraq, Qatar, and Turkey, for grave violations of religious freedom: laws on the registration of religions, enforcement of blasphemy, discrimination against minorities (source: USCIRF). Human Rights Watch documents a structural anti-Shiite bias in Saudi Arabia, unchanged after the advertised reforms of 2017-2018 (sources: HRW 2017, HRW 2018). Object level: actor. Class of proof: institutional, with the corresponding diplomatic bias.

### Religious minorities: Copts and Yazidis

The Copts of Egypt represent about 10% of the population (9.5 to 10 million people), with a range of sources from 6% to 18% — the government minimizes, the Church maximizes: a figure to be quoted with its range. In August 2013, after the removal of President Morsi, a wave of attacks destroyed about forty churches looted and burned and damaged twenty-three others — described as the worst violence against the Coptic Church since the fourteenth century; the police forced the victims into "reconciliations" with no prosecution of the attackers (source: Wikipedia, *Copts in Egypt*).

The genocide of the Yazidis of August 2014, perpetrated by the Islamic State at Sinjar, left about 5,000 dead and 6,800 abducted, mostly women and children reduced to sexual slavery; about 40,000 people took refuge on Mount Sinjar. The qualification of genocide was established by the UN investigative team UNITAD on May 10, 2021, with 1,444 identified alleged perpetrators, and recognized by German courts (Frankfurt, 2021), the Bundestag (2023), and the Paris assize court (March 20, 2026, life sentence for Sabri Essid). Thirteen states officially recognize this genocide in 2026 (sources: *Genocide of the Yazidis*, *Yazidi genocide*). It is the most solid figure in the whole corpus: forensic and judicial level.

### Anti-African xenophobia: a complete observable mechanism

The Tunisian case documents the entire chain, from discourse to population. On February 21, 2023, President Kais Saied declared that sub-Saharan "hordes of irregular migrants" were part of a "criminal plan to change the demographic composition" of the country. In the following weeks: widespread racist attacks, arbitrary detentions, evictions, job losses — affecting students and legal residents as well (sources: ReliefWeb, Soufan Center). An emerging nationalist party spread the narrative of Black migration conceived as a "Zionist colonial movement" meant to dismember Tunisia. Figures: camps of 7,000 migrants dismantled with forced deportations; the 2023 European Union-Tunisia memorandum, about 225 million dollars against migration, criticized as giving carte blanche to abuse (sources: Reuters, Middle East Eye). Since 2017 the IOM had documented the existence of open-air slave markets in Libya (source: *Slavery in Libya*).

The observation is here the most precious for the analysis: a conspiracy enunciated from above, relayed by a political current, multiplied by external funding, discharging into violence against populations with no decision-making power. The conspiratorial structure is complete, observable from end to end.

### Arabic-language antisemitism: hatred measured

The ADL Global 100 survey (collection July-November 2024, 103 countries) measures that 76% of respondents in the Middle East and North Africa adhere to at least six of the eleven antisemitic stereotypes, against 46% worldwide and 17% in Western Europe; the sixteen countries with the highest scores are all in the region; only 16% of respondents in the region acknowledge the historical accuracy of the Holocaust (sources: ADL, Factually).

Online spread is measured by two independent studies. The George Washington University study (six months after October 7, 2023) establishes a clear quantitative spike of antisemitic content in Arabic, with the sharpest rise on TikTok and the highest volume on X; decisive observation: the share of posts collectively accusing Jews (IHRA type 3 stereotype) remains stable — 23% before, 20% after — what changes is the volume, not the pretext (source: GWU). The CyberWell measure, published by the INSS, shows reported antisemitic content rising from 6,959 (September 11-October 6, 2023) to 12,949 (October 7-31), i.e. +86%, with a shift from dehumanizing stereotypes toward calls for violence and collective responsibility (source: INSS).

A convergence of two classes of method, survey and corpus: the population level and the discourse level are here the best established of the corpus.

## 4. The Methodological Fork: Concluding Without Essentializing {#s4}

Rigor requires distinguishing what the facts establish from what they do not allow us to conclude.

**Branch A — established.** Discriminatory legal devices exist (actor level: laws, courts, policies); documented extreme violence has been perpetrated against minorities (fact level: Yazidi genocide, anti-Coptic attacks); attitudes of hatred are measured at levels with no regional equivalent (population level: ADL); their digital spread is quantified (discourse level: GWU, INSS).

**Branch B — not established and not to be asserted.** That "the Arab world" or "Islam" are in themselves spaces of hatred is an essentializing conclusion that the data do not support: they document laws, currents, governments, and discourses, not an essence of a people or a faith. Moreover, the hatred of difference is neither statistically nor historically the monopoly of the region — Europe produced its most lethal forms in the twentieth century — and the region also contains its traditions of coexistence and its civil societies that document the abuses.

Discriminating observations between the two branches: systematically distinguish the faith from its ideologies; distinguish governments from populations; count hatred in all its directions; date each phenomenon instead of eternalizing it.

The thesis of this article is then precise: it is not Islam that failed, it is currents that turned its vocabulary against its ethic. *Kāfir*, the word that names the one who covers, designates in outline the movement of these ideologies: they cover the seed — the justice and individual choice that the Islam of the origins placed at the foundation — under a layer of communal hatred.

---

## 5. Conclusion {#s5}

The finding is documented: in part of the Arab world, difference is persecuted by laws, massacred by groups, hated at measured levels without equivalent, and its hatred circulates in growing quantities in digital corpora. But the most important finding is of another nature: none of this is necessary. A religion founded on individual choice, justice as the purpose of the law, and recognized disagreement as constitutive carries within it the exact resources of its own correction. The hatred of difference is not an original deposit to be accepted; it is a historical construction, datable, documented — hence, in principle, deconstructible.

The opening is here: the work of deradicalization does not consist in opposing the West to Islam, but in opposing, within the tradition itself, the ethic of the origins to the ideology of the present. The seed is not dead; it is covered. The work consists in digging it up.
