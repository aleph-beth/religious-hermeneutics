**Type**: Strategic analysis — literary transfer
**Thesis**: Dante's *Divine Comedy* (1320), founding text of European literature, draws from the Islamic imagination of the journey through the afterlife (*Miraj*). The thesis, launched by Miguel Asin Palacios in 1919, was first received as a nationalist scandal then progressively confirmed by the discovery of the *Book of Muhammad's Ladder* in Latin and Castilian translation. The most "European" work of world literature has Arab roots.

---

## 1. The thesis of Asin Palacios (1919) {#s1}

### The book

Miguel Asin Palacios, a Spanish priest and Arabist, publishes in 1919 *La escatologia musulmana en la Divina Comedia* — a 500-page tome that systematically compares Dante's *Commedia* with Islamic narratives of the afterlife.

### The parallels identified

| Element | Islamic tradition | Dante |
|---------|-------------------|-------|
| **Guided journey through the afterlife** | Muhammad guided by Gabriel in the Miraj | Dante guided by Virgil then Beatrice |
| **Hell in concentric circles** | *Kitab al-Miraj*: hell in descending tiers toward the center | Nine circles of Hell |
| **Paradise in ascending spheres** | Seven heavens of the Miraj (planets -> divine throne) | Nine celestial spheres -> Empyrean |
| **Purgatory as a mountain** | Certain versions of the Miraj: mountain between hell and paradise | Purgatory is a mountain |
| **Final divine light** | Nur Allah (light of God) at the summit of the journey | The "beatific vision" — divine light at the end of Paradise |
| **Encounter with the prophets** | Muhammad meets Adam, Jesus, Moses, Abraham in the heavens | Dante encounters figures in each sphere |
| **River of light** | River of light in Paradise (Quranic traditions) | *Fiume di luce* (Paradise, Canto XXX) |

### The scandalous reception

In 1919, the thesis provokes outrage: Italy is in the midst of post-Great War nationalism, and asserting that Dante owes something to the Arabs is perceived as an attack on European identity. The Italian Dante scholars (Gabrieli, Cerulli) reject the thesis — but the contestation will be progressively undermined by the evidence.

## 2. The *Book of Muhammad's Ladder*: the missing proof {#s2}

### The discovery

In 1949, the Orientalist Enrico Cerulli — who had initially fought against Asin Palacios — discovers the manuscripts of the **Liber Scalae Machometi**: a Latin and Castilian translation of the *Kitab al-Miraj*, produced at the court of **Alfonso X of Castile** (Toledo, ~1264).

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Source text** | *Kitab al-Miraj* (Arabic) — detailed account of Muhammad's night journey |
| **Translator** | Abraham of Toledo (Jewish), assisted by Bonaventura of Siena |
| **Patron** | Alfonso X the Wise, King of Castile |
| **Date** | ~1264 |
| **Languages** | Arabic -> Castilian -> Latin -> Old French |
| **Circulation** | The Latin text circulates in 13th-14th century Italy |

### Did Dante read the text?

The question remains debated. What is established:
- The *Liber Scalae* exists in Latin **before** the composition of the *Commedia* (1308-1320)
- The text circulates in Florentine and Bolognese intellectual circles
- Brunetto Latini, **Dante's teacher**, stayed in Spain and was familiar with the work of Alfonso X's court
- Dante himself places **Averroes, Avicenna, and Saladin** in Limbo (Inferno, Canto IV) — he knows Islamic culture and accords it exceptional respect

Proof of a direct reading does not exist. But the convergence of evidence is overwhelming.

## 3. Ibn Arabi: the Murcian mystic and the other source {#s3}

### The *Futuhat al-Makkiyya* and the mystical journey

Ibn Arabi (1165-1240), born in Murcia (Al-Andalus), is the greatest mystic of Islam. His descriptions of the mystical ascent through the celestial spheres in the *Futuhat al-Makkiyya* present even more precise parallels with Dante than the classical Miraj:

| Element from Ibn Arabi | Parallel in Dante |
|------------------------|-------------------|
| Journey through the planetary spheres | Identical structure of Paradise |
| Each sphere governed by a prophet | Each heaven has its specific inhabitants |
| The final vision as absolute light | The Empyrean as pure light |
| The feminine guide (*fata* / Sophia) | Beatrice as guide in Paradise |
| Love as the engine of ascension | *L'amor che move il sole e l'altre stelle* |

### The feminine figure

The most striking parallel: in Ibn Arabi, mystical love passes through a real woman — **Nizam** of Mecca, who inspires the *Tarjuman al-Ashwaq* (The Interpreter of Desires). In Dante, it is **Beatrice** — a real woman become spiritual guide. This structure (real woman -> salvific figure -> access to the divine) has no equivalent in the Latin Christian tradition but is central to Andalusian Sufism.

## 4. The overall argument {#s4}

The point is not to prove that Dante "copied" the Miraj. The argument is deeper: **the imaginary of the afterlife** that structures the *Commedia* — the circles, the spheres, the mountain, the light, the guide, the salvific woman — is an imaginary that does not exist in the Latin Christian tradition before contact with Islam.

Before the 13th century, the Christian afterlife consists of:
- **The Apocalypse of John**: prophetic visions, not a structured journey
- **Purgatory**: a theologically late concept (Third Lateran Council, 1179; developed by Jacques Le Goff, *The Birth of Purgatory*, 1981)
- **Visionary journeys** (Visio Tnugdali, ~1149): structurally simpler than the Miraj

The complex and systematic structure of the *Commedia* — descending hell, mountain of purgatory, paradise ascending through concentric spheres — has no Christian precedent. It has an Islamic one.

## 5. Current historiographical positioning {#s5}

| Position | Representative | Argument |
|----------|---------------|----------|
| **Direct transmission** | Asin Palacios (1919) | Parallels too numerous and too precise to be coincidental |
| **Indirect transmission** | Cerulli (1949, revised) | The *Liber Scalae* proves the existence of a channel — Dante does not need to have read the Arabic text directly |
| **Cultural atmosphere** | Menocal (1987) | The intellectual milieu of Florence/Bologna in the 13th c. is saturated with Arab knowledge — Dante is immersed in it |
| **Denial** | Certain traditional Dante scholars | Coincidences or common sources (Neoplatonism) |

The current consensus leans toward **indirect transmission**: Dante probably did not read the *Kitab al-Miraj* in Arabic, but the text existed in Latin within his intellectual environment, and the Islamic imaginary of the afterlife circulated through multiple channels (Toledo, Sicily, Crusades).

## 6. The thesis in three sentences {#s6}

The *Divine Comedy* — the founding text of European literature, the monument of Italian cultural identity — owes its cosmological structure to the Islamic imagination of the Miraj, transmitted through Toledo and the court of Alfonso X. It is not plagiarism but a fertilization: Dante takes an Islamic seed and makes it the greatest Christian poem. Europe at its most intimate — its literature — is still a daughter of Cordoba.
