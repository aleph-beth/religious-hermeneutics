## 1. The site {#s1}

Ephesus lies on the west coast of Asia Minor (modern Turkey), facing the Aegean Sea in the valley of the Cayster, a few kilometres from present-day Selçuk. The city grew around a harbour that has since silted up, which explains its slow decline: the sea has retreated as far as 7 kilometres from the present ruins. The site unfolds between two mountains that structure all local belief.

---

## 2. The two mountains {#s2}

### Mount Pion (Panayır Dağı)

Lying to the north-east, it borders the ancient city directly. It is the mountain of the resurrection story.

- The cave of the Seven Sleepers opens on its flank: according to Christian tradition, seven young Christians slept there for nearly two centuries before waking under Theodosius II.
- The Qur'an (surat al-Kahf) tells the story of the People of the Cave (*Ashāb al-Kahf*), with the dog Qitmir lying on the threshold.
- Local folklore calls the cave the "witch's cave": a healer is said to have performed cures there since ancient times.
- Symbolic function: the mountain of sleep and waking, of passage from death to life, of refuge from persecution.

### Mount Coressos (Bülbül Dağı, "Nightingale Hill")

It closes the valley to the south. It is the mountain of the divine dwelling.

- The House of the Virgin Mary (*Meryem Ana*) stands on its flank, a pilgrimage site shared by Christians and Muslims, revealed in the nineteenth century through the visions of Anne Catherine Emmerich.
- The Basilica of St John rises at its foot, over the presumed tomb of the apostle.
- The Isa Bey Mosque (1375) stands nearby, between the basilica and the Temple of Artemis.
- Symbolic function: the mountain of the sacred dwelling, of continuity from one tradition to the next.

---

## 3. The valley: the city and the goddess {#s3}

Between the two mountains lies the ancient city itself:

- **The Artemision**, the Temple of Artemis and one of the Seven Wonders of the World, stood on the plain outside the city: the tutelary goddess watched over the city from her own domain.
- **The Curetes Street** links the heights to the urban core; the Curetes, mythical dancing priests, clashed their shields to drown out the cries of Leto giving birth to Artemis and to distract Hera.
- **The Great Theatre** (24,000 seats): the scene of the riot against St Paul ("Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!", Acts 19).
- **The Fountain of Trajan and the Temple of Hadrian** align the imperial cult along the processional axis.

---

## 4. Structural reading: a topography on three levels {#s4}

| Level | Places | Religious function |
|---|---|---|
| The plain | Artemision, harbour, civic city | Civic cult of the tutelary goddess |
| Pion | Cave of the Seven Sleepers | Refuge, resurrection, protection |
| Coressos | Meryem Ana, Basilica of St John, Isa Bey Mosque | Dwelling of the sacred, pilgrimage, continuity |

The geography of Ephesus tells a complete religious history: civic cult in the valley, cults of refuge and pilgrimage on the mountains.
