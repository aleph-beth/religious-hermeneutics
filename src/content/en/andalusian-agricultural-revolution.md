## The Islamic "Green Revolution" (Andrew Watson, 1974)

Historian Andrew Watson identifies a genuine agricultural revolution in the medieval Islamic world, of which Al-Andalus is the western epicenter. This revolution rests on three pillars:

### 1. The introduction of new crops

The Arabs transported plants from India, Persia, and East Africa to the western Mediterranean. Al-Andalus became the laboratory for this acclimatization:

| Introduced crop | Origin | European word (< Arabic) | Impact |
|-----------------|--------|--------------------------|--------|
| **Rice** | Southeast Asia | *arroz* < *al-ruzz* | Staple food in the Mediterranean |
| **Sugarcane** | India | *azucar* < *al-sukkar* | Foundation of the colonial economy (Caribbean, Brazil) |
| **Cotton** | India | *algodon* < *al-qutn* | European textile revolution |
| **Bitter orange** | China/India | *naranja* < *naranj* | Mediterranean landscape |
| **Lemon** | Asia | *limon* < *laymun* | Diet, medicine (scurvy) |
| **Eggplant** | India | *berenjena* < *badhinjān* | Mediterranean cuisine |
| **Spinach** | Persia | *espinaca* < *isfanakh* | Diet |
| **Artichoke** | Maghreb | *alcachofa* < *al-kharshuf* | European cuisine |
| **Saffron** | Persia | *azafran* < *al-za'faran* | Most expensive spice in the world — La Mancha is today its leading producer |
| **Apricot** | China/Persia | *albaricoque* < *al-barquq* | Mediterranean arboriculture |
| **Watermelon** | Africa | *sandia* (from Arabic *sindiyya*) | Summer diet |

### 2. Systematic irrigation

Al-Andalus transformed the inherited Roman irrigation systems into a sophisticated network:

| Technique | Name (< Arabic) | Description |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------|
| **Acequia** | *al-saqiya* | Main irrigation canal — the Valencia network still functions today |
| **Noria** | *na'ura* | Hydraulic lifting wheel — enables terrace irrigation |
| **Alberca** | *al-birka* | Water retention basin |
| **Aljibe** | *al-jubb* | Underground cistern |
| **Azud** | *al-sudd* | Diversion dam |
| **Acena** | *al-saniya* | Hydraulic mill |

The **Tribunal de las Aguas** of Valencia, which meets every Thursday in front of the cathedral since the 10th century, is a juridical institution directly inherited from Islamic water law. It is inscribed on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage list.

### 3. Agronomy as a science

Al-Andalus produced the first systematic agronomy treatises of Western Europe:

| Agronomist | Period | Work | Contribution |
|------------|--------|------|-------------|
| **Ibn Bassal** | 11th c. (Toledo) | *Kitab al-Filaha* | Soil classification, grafting techniques, rotations |
| **Ibn al-'Awwam** | 12th c. (Seville) | *Kitab al-Filaha* (580 chapters) | Most comprehensive encyclopedia: 585 plants, 50 fruit trees, livestock, beekeeping |
| **Abu l-Khayr** | 11th c. (Seville) | Botanical treatise | Plant taxonomy |
| **Ibn Luyun** | 14th c. (Almeria) | Agronomic poem | Last great Andalusian synthesis |

Ibn al-'Awwam's *Kitab al-Filaha* was not translated into Spanish until **1802** — seven centuries after it was written. Throughout that time, his techniques were practiced without their source being acknowledged.

## The colonial paradox: sugarcane

The sugarcane chain is the most striking example of the reception-expulsion-conquest mechanism applied to agriculture:

```
India (sugarcane cultivation)
    -> Persia (sugar refining)
        -> Arab world (Mediterranean diffusion)
            -> Al-Andalus (acclimatization in Spain, 10th c.)
                -> Reconquista (Christians inherit the plantations)
                    -> Madeira and Canaries (first colonial trials, 15th c.)
                        -> Brazil (1530+) and Caribbean (17th c.)
                            -> PLANTATION SLAVERY
                                -> Modern Atlantic economy
```

Sugar — the product that justified the slave trade and plantation slavery — is an Islamic agronomic knowledge, transmitted through Al-Andalus, turned back as an instrument of colonial domination.

## The Spanish landscape as memory

Present-day Spain is an Andalusian agricultural palimpsest:

- The **orange groves** of Valencia and Seville
- The **rice paddies** of the Albufera (Valencia)
- The **saffron** of La Mancha
- The **irrigation systems** of the Huerta de Murcia and Valencia
- The **olive groves** systematized by Andalusian grafting techniques
- The **gardens** of the Alhambra and the Generalife

Tourists who admire the "Spanish Mediterranean landscape" are contemplating an Arab landscape.

## Summary: what Andalusian agriculture changed in Europe

| Before Al-Andalus | After Al-Andalus |
|--------------------|-----------------|
| Subsistence farming, one harvest per year | Multiple rotations, two to three harvests per year |
| Declining Roman irrigation | Sophisticated and maintained hydraulic network |
| Monotonous diet (cereals, meat) | Mediterranean dietary diversity |
| No cash crops | Sugar, cotton, saffron as economic drivers |
| Empirical agronomy, no treatises | Systematic agronomic science |
